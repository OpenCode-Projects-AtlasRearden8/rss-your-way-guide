# Chapter 5: The Triage Habit

Everything so far has been setup. This chapter is the actual practice: a small daily ritual that takes the place of the scroll, guilt-free, on good days and bad ones.

## The problem with "clean your inbox"

The number-one habit people attempt here is *"clear all unread."* It works like a shot of espresso: great for three days, then the pile blurs, and clearing becomes its own doomscroll. So this chapter proposes something kinder.

**The goal is not zero unread. The goal is one honest pass.**

You are not the maintenance department for your own inbox. You're the editor of it. Editors triage; they don't dutifully read everything that crosses the desk.

## The three-pass triage (5 minutes, done once, then stop)

When you sit down with the reader — morning coffee, evening wind-down, whatever slot you have — you make **three tiny passes**:

**Pass 1: The triage (no reading).** Walk each folder, item by item: title, maybe first sentence. For each one say one of three words out loud or in your head:

- **Keep** — "this sounds good, I'll actually read it soon"
- **Skim** — "mild interest; one glance now, and it's done"
- **Drop** — "read that headline; I know enough"

**Pass 2: The skim (boundary).** Go back to your *Skim* items only. Read enough that you'd have something to say if they came up at a table with friends. Then mark them read. Done. Skim items are not deferred; deferring them forever is how piles grow.

**Pass 3: One deep dive (optional).** Time and interest permitting — and only if permitting — pick **one** *Keep* item and actually read it. One. This is the deep, slow part of reading; it doesn't even have to happen every day.

Then **close the reader.** That's the ritual. It fits a coffee. It fits a bus ride. It fits a bad day.

## The rules that keep it kind

1. **Three passes, one sitting, then out.** No "let me just check Twitter to see if I missed something." You didn't miss something. That's the point of choosing your sources.
2. **No guilt about Drop.** "Drop" is a judgment about the item, not about you. You will drop most items, most items are fine to drop. That's triage; it's supposed to feel a bit brutal and 100% effective.
3. **Never process two folders at once.** One folder, three passes, next folder or stop. Fatigue is where triage goes bad.
4. **Mark-binary, not a mood.** Each item ends *read* or *unread with a plan.* There is no "I'm going to get to [it]," because that's where the pile breeds. Unread-with-no-plan means read it (skim) or drop it (drop). Pick a word.
5. **The pile is allowed to grow overnight.** Skip a day, and 60 new things are sitting there in the morning. That happened. You triage them once, drop most, and move on. The ritual absorbs growth; that's what triage is *for*.

## Replacing the scroll, specifically

Compare the feel of the two habits:

| | The scroll | The triage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start point | Opened by impulse, or by a notification | Opened by intention, at a slot you chose |
|Contents | Whatever someone else put there | Your chosen sources |
| End point | Doesn't exist | 5 minutes, and out |
| Afterglow | Worse than before: anxious, behind | Neutral: informed, done |
| Daily cost | An hour, invisibly | 5 minutes, visibly |

If you get the itch to scroll — the *reach for the phone* reflex — the substitute is the same physical motion: open the reader instead, triage one folder, close it. The reflex wants motion in the hands and a little novelty. Triage gives both, and stops.

And if you *miss* the noise of a big feed, a couple of contented notes:

- A few feeds are fine as noise. You subscribe to them (a *Quick reads* folder can hold one or two), and you process them the same way. Noise on your terms has a stop button built in.
- You may find, after a couple of weeks, that you're *less* informed about junk and *more* informed about the specific things you care about. Nobody regrets this trade.

## Fallback for bad weeks

ADHD-friendly explicit version, because the guide promised one: **on the worst week of a bad month, the triage is two words.**

1. Open each folder. Skim nothing. Look at every new title. One word per item: **Drop** or **Skim**.
2. Skim nothing. Clear nothing else. Close.

That's passing 1 only, no passes 2 and 3. It takes two minutes and it keeps the pile moving so it doesn't snowball into an Avoidance Object. A two-minute triage on bad weeks beats a perfect system you quit in February. The system is allowed to shrink; it is not allowed to abandon you.

## One last note

This chapter can feel like too little structure or too much, depending on the day. Both readings are fine. The 3 passes are a default, not a law. The single non-negotiable thing is just: **the reader stays finite, the sources stay yours, and you visit on purpose.** Everything else is adjustable furniture.
