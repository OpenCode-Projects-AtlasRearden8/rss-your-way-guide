# Chapter 2: Choosing a Free Reader

A feed reader is the only app you need for this whole system. Below are honest, opinionated options — all free at the level you need to start, all without ads pushed into your face.

> **A note on honesty:** free reader apps have to fund themselves somehow. The common pattern is a free tier (you + your feeds, which covers 95% of people) and a paid tier (fancy features, team stuff). Nothing below requires payment or pressure to buy.

## The map

| Reader | Platforms | Syncs across devices? | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **NetNewsWire** | Apple (iPhone, iPad, Mac) | Optional (via your own iCloud or blogsy-style sync) | Apple users; the calmest experience | Apple-only; no Android |
| **Feedbro** | Desktop browser (Firefox, Chrome, Edge) | Browser-based | People who want everything in the browser they already use | Interface has quirks; local-only unless configured |
| **FreshRSS** | Self-hosted web app | Yes, if you set it up | Privacy-first people comfortable following a setup guide (even gently) | Requires somewhere to host it — skip until chapters 1–5 make sense |
| **Feeder (free tier)** | Android | Yes | Android users who want simple | Free tier caps feeds — fine to start |
| **[Your podcast app]** | Everywhere | Yes | Podcasts only (they *are* feed readers) | See chapter 3 — reuse what you already have |

Recommendations here are plain software suggestions, not affiliate links. Every reader listed supports standard feeds (RSS/Atom) without locking you in.

## The three questions that actually matter

Ask yourself these, in order:

1. **Where will I actually read?** Kindle-paperwork-late-in-bed? A phone reader wins — that's still fine, because a reader phone is sterile compared to a social app. Mostly at a laptop? Feedbro or NetNewsWire on Mac.
2. **Do I want one inbox for everything?** In that case pick an app that handles both articles and podcasts (many do lately), or deliberately keep podcast feeds in your podcast app and text feeds in your reader. Both work. Chapter 3 covers the split.
3. **Will this hold for years?** All four suggestions here can *export all your subscriptions as an OPML file*. That's the escape hatch that means "no one owns you." We'll use it in chapter 4.

## Our gentle default

For most people starting out on this path, at most energy points, we say:

- **Apple household?** NetNewsWire. It's genuinely free, respectful, and calm.
- **Other phone or laptop?** Pick Feeder (Android) or Feedbro (browser). Either is a two-minute setup. Switching later costs nothing because of OPML export.

## A 10-minute starter exercise

1. Install the reader that matches where you'll actually sit (not the "best" one on someone's list — the one that's easiest where *you* are).
2. Subscribe to **one** feed you already love. Any blog or newsletter that sends you actual emails; check for an "RSS" or "Subscribe" link in its footer.
3. Read it *right now* for two minutes in the reader. Notice that nothing chirped at you to do it.
4. Close the reader. That's the whole exercise. The habit comes in chapter 5.

That's it. If one reader doesn't click, it is not a failure — it's data. Export OPML, try another, keep your subscriptions. The point of the whole guide is that *you* own the list, not the app.
